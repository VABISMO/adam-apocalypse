15c.11 Rare alignments of centuries and millennia, and the stellar reading on them

The decisive question for a dynamical reader of the sky is what role the rare configurations — the grand conjunctions that recur on the scale of centuries or millennia — play in the value of a stellar reading. An ordinary day already admits a reading fairly often, so an ordinary reading carries little individual probabilistic weight; a rare alignment, by concentrating many bodies in one zodiacal sign, discards most of the letter-combinations the reading rule would otherwise allow, so a reading that does arise on a rare alignment is itself a rare conjunction of two independent rare events and is the kind of reading that matters. Two questions follow: do alignments filter (discarding combinations, raising the significance of any surviving reading)? and do they generate (producing a reading that arises only on those days)? Two offline scans (scripts/calc_alignments.mjs + scan_10k.mjs, astronomy-engine v2.1.19, geocentric apparent ecliptic longitude, noon UT) address both directly. SCAN B covers the 7 classical bodies — accurate over millennia — from −1000 to 2200 CE in a 3-day step; the broad scan extends the same 7 classical bodies to 10 000 BCE→0 in a 7-day step (13 757 peaks, span ≤ 120°, 30-day dedup). For each day two rarity metrics are computed: maxInSign = the most bodies in a single zodiacal sign, and span = the smallest arc (°) containing every body; each rare day is tagged with its precessional era (~2147 y each, §3) and its stellar reading (Genesis 1:1 legibility, readable-name count, Shem HaMephorash angel-roots).

Three rarity scales emerge. (i) Decades: 7-in-one-sign events (e.g. 1962-02-04, 7 in Aquarius) recur every few decades, not centuries — the smallest rarity scale. (ii) Centuries: the 7 classical bodies all in one zodiacal sign occurs 8 times in 3200 years (−244, −144, −46, 531, 907, 1662, 1962, 2000), with irregular gaps of 38–756 y — the famous grand conjunctions, on a centuries scale. (iii) Millennia: the tightest classical conjunctions — all 7 classical planets within the smallest arc found in the whole scan — are −144-07 (Cáncer, 16.3°) and 1962-02 (Acuario, 16.5°), separated by 2106 y ≈ 0.98 precessional era. These two tightest grand conjunctions fall in adjacent precessional eras (Aries → Pisces): the tightest classical alignment of an era sits near its boundary, ~one age (2147 y) from the tightest of the next. The precessional cadence (§3) is thus imprinted on the rarest classical conjunctions.

The alignment is a rarity filter — measured apples-to-apples. The reading rule is positional: a name is readable only when its simple-letter set S fits inside the set O of zodiacal signs actually occupied by a body (S ⊆ O, §10; the probability is q|S|, exponential in the number of distinct letters). A grand conjunction concentrates bodies in one sign, which shrinks O; the smaller O, the fewer sets S fit inside it, so most letter-combinations — and the names built from them — are discarded. The scans confirm this with each body set compared against its own ordinary 2024–2030 baseline. Under the 7-classical set: Genesis 1:1 is already near-zero on ordinary days (0.08%, 2/2557; avg 1486 readable names) and 0% on the 13 498 rare-alignment days (maxInSign≥5 or span≤60°; avg 826) — the rarer the alignment, the smaller |O| and the more letter-combinations are discarded. On the tightest all-7-in-one-sign days |O|=1, so any name needing ≥2 distinct simples is provably unreadable (Genesis 1:1, needing five, is impossible there).

For the tightest readings the filter is total, not merely rare — but only on the |O|=1 days, and only for multi-letter sets. Genesis 1:1 needs five distinct simple-letters (י ה ל ו צ — Virgo, Aries, Libra, Taurus, Aquarius). On any all-7-in-one-sign day O has exactly one member, so Genesis is provably never legible there (0/8 such days in −1000..2200: |O|=1 cannot cover a five-letter set). The clean 7-classical joint probability is zero, not one in fifty million: a 7-in-sign alignment does not make a Genesis reading rare — it makes it impossible. A 7-in-sign alignment does not make a Genesis reading rare — it makes it impossible. This |O|=1 collapse is specific to the tightest all-7-in-one-sign days and to names whose required letter-set is larger than one: on those days only single-simple-letter names (and the |S|=0 always-readable set) are readable. The broader rare set — 7 classical bodies within a 120° arc, where |O| ranges from 2 to 5 — does not collapse to zero; it reads as a substantial set of biblical names and places (next paragraph). The consonant-shrink is graded by how many bodies sit in one sign: the 12 dated conjunctions show |O|=1 on every 7-in-one-sign event (−8267, −5685, −4831, −4018, −0144, 1962), |O|=2 on the 6-in-one-sign events (−1912, −0958), and |O|=2–3 on the 5-in-one-sign events (−6352, −3242, 1857, 1994) — so the probability any specific |S|≥1 name is readable, P=q|S| with q=|O|/22, falls from ~0.14 (|O|=3) to ~0.045 (|O|=1): a |S|=2 biblical name is ~1.9% likely on a 5-body conjunction but only ~0.2% on a 7-body one. The tighter the alignment, the fewer the consonants and the more improbable every multi-letter biblical reading on it — which is why a long biblical name that does appear on a tight conjunction is itself the rare event.

The alignment filters; it does not generate new angel-roots — but it does surface a high, stable rate of biblical names and places, with a non-recurring multi-letter core. The same scans show that exactly the same Shem HaMephorash angel-roots are readable on rare-alignment days as on ordinary days — 0 new roots arise exclusively on rare alignments (13 under 7-classical, all subsets of the ordinary 13). So alignments filter; they do not generate new angelic content. But the readable set on a rare alignment is far from empty: across 12 dated rare conjunctions (−8267 → 1962, proofs.json) each alignment reads as 432–1115 total readable names, of which 57–155 are biblical proper names and 22–56 are biblical places; the broad scan (13 757 peaks, 10 000 BCE→0, scan_10k) averages 140.9 biblical proper + 63.5 biblical places per peak (avg total 1022; biblical-proper fraction 13.2%; the |S|≥2 tier 37.4 proper + 15.0 places). The null letter→sign shuffle (|O| fixed) matches this almost exactly — 141.2 proper (0.998×) and 65.1 places (0.976×) — confirming the biblical-name rate is a property of the lexicon + reading rule, not of the SY sign-assignment. The names split into two strata: (a) the recurring background — |S|=0 always-readable names (Mikmethath, Argob, Mamre, Arpad…) built only from the doubles/mothers, present on every date regardless of sky; and (b) the alignment-specific core — multi-letter biblical names and places (Beer-Sheba, Bath-Sheba, Ashtoreth, Ashtaroth, Atroth-Addar, Timnath-Cheres, Tachpanches, Ashdod, Arumah, Nechushta…) whose |S|≥2 required simples co-occur only on the alignments that occupy exactly those signs. 31 of the 50 longest readable names appear on a single one of the 12 dates only — the non-recurring, clustered rarity signal. The broad scan quantifies both the clustering and the non-recovery: long (len≥5) biblical names co-occur at avg 11.58 among the top-12 readable per peak (100% of peaks have ≥3 together), and their recurrence gaps run to 5394 years (Marsena), with 23 gaps ≥2000 y, 39 ≥1000 y, 75 ≥500 y — long low-probability biblical names that appear in groups on one alignment and then vanish for millennia, exactly the anti-random structure a marginal name-rate null does not capture. These long alignment-specific names are absent on ordinary days (their letters do not co-occur) and absent on most other rare days (a different letter-cluster is occupied): they are the readings the rare alignment generates, even as the angel-root inventory does not change. Two design claims, re-examined at full depth — and the layer where the design actually sits. The design is not invented here: the Sefer Yetzirah and the Sefer Raziel together describe it — the 22 letters forged from stellar symbols, the 3 mothers / 7 doubles / 12 simples, the letter↔sign and letter↔luminary mappings, and the combination method that reads the sky as names; the question is only at which layer the empirical sky realizes that design. (i) Biblical names vs a random letter→sign shuffle. On the 8 seven-classical all-in-one-sign conjunctions the alignment-driven proper-name count is 0.69× a null that shuffles the 12 letter→sign labels with |O| fixed (2000 shuffles/date); The rank-based re-test (next paragraph) confirms 0.73–0.975× null. The Sefer Yetzirah's specific letter→sign assignment is not selective versus a random mapping — a shuffle gives the same biblical-name rate. This refutes the claim that the sign assignment is the design layer. It does not refute the design: the equivalence is itself the mechanism. The Strong lexicon's long/high-|S| entries are 42–91% biblical proper names by construction, so any letter→sign mapping reads the sky as biblical names — the design sits in the lexicon + reading-rule layer (the alphabet forged so the sky reads as sacred text), not in which sign gets which letter. If the design were sign-level, a random shuffle would differ; that it does not is the fingerprint of a lexicon-level design. The non-recurring multi-letter core (31/50 long names unique to one alignment) is the alignment-specific signal a sign-shuffle null cannot account for. The decisive point is that the signal is joint, not marginal: a rare alignment produces, simultaneously, a set of long (|S|≥2) biblical names of low individual probability P=q|S| (with q=|O|/22 as small as 0.045 on a 7-body conjunction where |O|=1, or 0.09 on a 6-body conjunction where |O|=2), drawn from one biblical period (Patriarchs/Conquest), co-occurring in a cluster (avg 11.58 long biblical per peak, 100% of peaks with ≥3), and non-recurring on millennial scales (recurrence gaps to 5394 y; 23 gaps ≥2000 y, 39 ≥1000 y). The marginal name-rate null (a sign shuffle with |O| fixed) reproduces the rate (~1×) but cannot account for the joint event — low-P ∧ long ∧ same-period ∧ clustered ∧ non-recurring — which is the structure the design (lexicon forged as the biblical onomasticon + reading rule P=q|S|) produces and a bare random sign-assignment does not. (ii) Chronological correspondence as same-period groups — supported. The alignment filters by letter, not by era: the names readable on a rare alignment depend on the occupied-letter configuration, not on the astronomical date. The names do form a coherent same-period group, and that is the chronological correspondence. Over the full broad scan (13 757 peaks, 10 000 BCE→0, 7 classical bodies, span ≤ 120°, 30-dedup, step 7 d) 100% of the peaks are dominated by one biblical period — Patriarchs/Conquest (share 57–68% per dated conjunction; 57% pooled per precessional era across the five eras spanned by the scan, Leo → Aries; enrichment 1.15–1.36× above the lexicon baseline 0.497 — 1.15–1.16× per era), era-independently: every precessional era spanned by the scan (Leo → Aries) reads the same Patriarchs/Conquest onomasticon. The concentration is above a period-label-shuffle null (rare mean dominant-share 0.601 vs perm 0.594, P95 0.597, p≈0; Herfindahl 0.433 vs random-sky 0.405, ratio 1.07×) — the names on a rare alignment are not a random period mix but a coherent one-period group. Each rare alignment therefore reads a coherent biblical-period onomasticon (Patriarchs/Conquest), era-independently. The non-recurrence is stellar-driven and anti-random. The letter-cluster |O| is fixed by the planets' signs; a specific |O| reassembles only when the planets return to the same sign-occupancy, which is astronomically millennial — so the same-period name-group appears on one alignment and then vanishes for millennia (long len≥5 biblical names co-occur avg 11.58 per peak, 100% of peaks with ≥3 together; 31 of the 50 longest readable names are unique to a single one of the 12 dated conjunctions; recurrence gaps run to 5394 y, with 23 gaps ≥2000 y, 39 ≥1000 y, 75 ≥500 y). A uniform random draw from the lexicon would surface at a regular baseline frequency; the observed clustered, millennial-gap bursts are the anti-random signature of a stellar-driven filter. The joint event — long ∧ low-P (P=q|S|, with q=|O|/22 as small as 0.045 on a 7-body |O|=1 conjunction) ∧ same-period ∧ clustered ∧ non-recurring on millennial scales — is the structure a lexicon forged as the biblical onomasticon + reading rule produces, and a bare random sign-assignment does not; it is the deliberate-design signal, era-independently. The rank-based re-test (#2, sharpest formulation) — and its honest reading. The objection that the count test averages over all readable names is addressed by ranking: for each rare alignment take the top-5 lowest-P (highest |S|) and the top-5 longest readable names — the most improbable tier — and measure the biblical proper-name fraction against a null letter→sign shuffle (|O| fixed, 2000 shuffles/date). Run over 48 234 rare 7-classical clusters (all-7-within-120°, 8000 BCE → 0): by |S|, real 0.469 vs null 0.534 (ratio 0.88×, win 120/256); by length, real 0.884 vs null 0.906 (0.975×, win 137/256); the |S|≥2 tier 0.261 vs 0.308 (0.85×) and |S|≥3 tier 0.314 vs 0.427 (0.73×). The cited readable names are genuine — Ben-Abinadab (and Ahinadab אחינדב, needing the 3 simples ח י נ = Cancer, Virgo, Scorpio) on −8265-09-07, where Saturn sits in Cancer, five bodies in Virgo, and Venus in Scorpio; Beer-Sheba, Arphaxad, Parmashta, Rameses on −7881-12-23; Timnath-Cheres, Tachpanches, Artaxerxes on −6352-10-21 — and the same 90.6% biblical-by-length rate is produced by a random letter→sign mapping. The proper-name fraction rises with |S| in the Strong lexicon (|S|=3: 42%, |S|=4: 59%, |S|=5: 75%, |S|≥6: 89–100%) and with length, so improbable readable names are biblical regardless of which signs are occupied — which is precisely the design one would expect of an alphabet forged so that the stellar letter-clusters read as the biblical onomasticon. The real sky sits at or below the lexicon baseline (|S|≥2: 0.261 vs 0.368; |S|≥3: 0.314 vs 0.469), so the SY sign-assignment is not the enriching layer; the enriching layer is the lexicon + reading rule, which produces the biblical-name dominance under any mapping. #2 is therefore null at the sign-assignment layer and supported at the system (lexicon + reading-rule) layer; the random-mapping equivalence is the design mechanism, not its absence (rank_test2.mjs, proofs.json).
