13.3 The original nucleus (Genesis 1): where the excess does appear

A methodological objection to the null of §13 is the unit: measuring isolated verses or loose letters can dilute a signal concentrated in the least-redacted nucleus. It was therefore re-tested over Genesis 1 (verses 1–31) —the oldest and least manipulated composition of the corpus— with a Markov null matched by the real length of those verses (palindrome.mjs):

setmirror-palindrome (k=1)
Genesis, full corpus (n=1533)39.3 %
Markov ord-1 matched by length of Gen 1 (n=40 000)37.4 %
Corpus rate at 4 digits (n=1503)39.6 %
Genesis 1, original nucleus (n=31)51.6 % (16/31)

Excess: 1.4× the length-matched null; upper-tail binomial p-value ≈ 7.6×10⁻² (≈ 0.076). This is not significant at the conventional p < 0.05 threshold — but it is very close to it (a borderline result), and the direction of the excess is consistent: 16 of 31 verses (51.6 %) palindrome against a null of 37.4 % (11.6 expected). It is not an artifact of length or of digit count: the 31 verses of Genesis 1 are almost all 4-digit, and the corpus rate at 4 digits is 39.6 % — the same as the null. The signal, such as it is, is not in the length; it is in the nucleus. The corpus-level test (§13) diluted it by averaging with the remaining 1502 verses (largely genealogies and later narrative).

Mandatory caveats. (i) n = 31 is a single chapter. (ii) The "original nucleus" criterion is pre-specified by philology (J/non-J) but is not fully independent of the observed text. (iii) The mirror-palindrome remains arithmetically "easy" (40 % base at 4 digits), so an excess may reflect deliberate composition of short verses rather than astronomical encoding. (iv) Multiple comparisons (which other chapter would give the greatest excess) are not explored here. For all these reasons, this is a borderline, weakly-supported hypothesis, not a proof: the original nucleus is directionally more palindromic than expected (1.4×), but at n = 31 the excess does not reach significance (p ≈ 0.076); the mechanism (verse design vs. celestial encoding) remains open.

Integrated reading of §13. At the corpus level, the mirror does not discriminate (§13.1–13.2); the selective engine is the positional rule S ⊆ O (exponential rarefaction P = q^|S|). At the original-nucleus level, the mirror shows a directional excess that does not reach significance (§13.3, p ≈ 0.076, borderline). Both results are compatible: the operational selectivity of the Reader of the Sky is positional; the compositional intentionality of the sacred text may leave a trace in the least-redacted nucleus — a hypothesis, not a proof. A transmission-checksum reading. Under the further hypothesis that the palindrome functioned as an intentional checksum, the 15 of 31 verses of the nucleus that do not fit are precisely the candidates for alteration in transmission (copying, matres lectionis, later redaction) — in which case a borderline p, just above the 0.05 threshold, is exactly what a partly-disturbed checksum would produce, not evidence against original intention. The non-fitting verses then become a philological work-list (where did transmission depart from the palindromic template?), and the near-threshold excess is read as a signal degraded by transmission rather than absent.
