15b.2 Aiq Bekar = the decimal-positional gematria of §2

The composition of a sigil requires reducing each letter to a value 1–9 that fits the 3×3 kamea. The traditional reduction is Aiq Bekar (איק בכר): recursive digit-sum. Verified: Aiq Bekar coincides exactly with the digit-sum of the gematria of §2 for the 27 letters (calc_magic_squares.mjs). The 9 groups gather the letters by positional digit:

1: א(1) י(10) ק(100)2: ב(2) כ(20) ר(200)3: ג(3) ל(30) ש(300)
4: ד(4) מ(40) ת(400)5: ה(5) נ(50) ך(500)6: ו(6) ס(60) ם(600)
7: ז(7) ע(70) ן(700)8: ח(8) פ(80) ף(800)9: ט(9) צ(90) ץ(900)

This 3×3 grid is the structure 9 = 9+9+9 of §2. Without decimal-positional gematria there is no Aiq Bekar, and without Aiq Bekar no sigil: the reduction is the bridge §2 ↔ operative tradition of sigils. Dating caveat: the Aiq Bekar reduction is medieval (attested in Midrash Tannaim, Abulafia, Baal ha-Turim; Greek root pythmen), but the name "Aiq Bekar", the 3×3 table, and above all the sigil-traced-on-the-kamea method are Renaissance (first Agrippa, De occulta philosophia III, 1531), not medieval Jewish; the Aiq Bekar↔Lo Shu link is modern (Golden Dawn, 19th c.).
