What the article does NOT claim: it does not claim an eclipse-Genesis causality (negative, §9.3), it does not claim that the mirror selects Genesis (negative, §13, also word level 47.9 % and phrase level 39.3 %), it does not claim that the mirror-palindrome is a textual checksum or the validity criterion of the readings of the sky (falsified: high 48 % base, and it does not separate legible from invalid days, 24.9 % vs 41.1 %, §13.1–13.2), it does not claim that Toric gematria encodes astronomy as a proven fact (it is a hypothesis within an attested tradition, §6.3). It does not claim to demonstrate that the stellar-letter technique predicts the future; it documents that the technique was historically used as divination (attested in the Sefer Yetzirah, Sefer Raziel, and Gaffarel, §15c.8/§15c.10/§1) and replicates it, without asserting its efficacy. Nor does it claim that the Sefer Yetzirah's specific letter→sign assignment is selective — it is not: a random letter→sign shuffle yields the same biblical-proper rate (count 0.69× null, win 2/8; rank-based re-test over 48 234 rare clusters 8000 BCE→0: top-5 by |S| 0.88× null and by length 0.975× null). Nor does it claim genealogical continuity — that biblical father→son pairs co-occur on the alignments above chance: tested on 34 attested pairs / 41 attested patriarchs (web/data/biblical_genealogy.json), the full 13 757-peak scan gives 5358 observed vs 6788 expected (0.79×, p=1.00 — sub-random); a son's proper name is coined independently of the father's, so genealogical continuity is not a leg of the proof (the demonstrated correspondence is same-period, not genealogical — see the nsCheckTable and §15c.11a). The design the article does claim sits one layer deeper: the lexicon + reading rule are constructed so that the sky reads as a high, stable rate of biblical names and places under any sign mapping (140.9 proper + 63.5 places per peak over 13 757 peaks, 10 000 BCE→0; 60–155 proper and 22–56 places per dated conjunction; the long/high-|S| entries of the Strong lexicon are 42–91% biblical proper names by construction), with a core of alignment-specific multi-letter biblical names (avg 8.80 long biblical co-occurring per peak, recurrence gaps to 5181 y) as the non-recurring, clustered, same-period rarity signal. It DOES claim chronological correspondence: each rare alignment reads a coherent same-period biblical onomasticon (Patriarchs/Conquest, 100% of 13 757 peaks 10 000 BCE→0, era-independent; concentration above a period-label-shuffle null, p≈0) — a group chronologically corresponding to the Bible with its own era, independent of the real astronomical date. (Gaffarel's Persian parchments attest the practice historically; they are not a chronological marker.)

What the article DOES claim (with evidence): intentional letter engineering is a historically attested practice (SY, Abulafia, Raziel) and, for the 72-letter verses of Exodus 14:19-21, empirically improbable by chance (p ≈ 5×10⁻⁷). The distinction between (a) intention of the practice [demonstrated] and (b) specific astronomical attribution of Toric gematria [hypothesis] is the epistemic core of the work. The stellar-letter reading was, historically, a divinatory technique — attested across cultures (Persian, Hebrew) and documented in the Sefer Yetzirah, Sefer Raziel, and Gaffarel (1629/1650); the apparatus replicates it from those instructions. The article documents this historical use; it does not assert that the technique in fact predicts the future.
