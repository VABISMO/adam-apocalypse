Secondary Monarchy (David/Solomon) cluster — the correspondence is not exclusive to Patriarchs. On the broader |O|≥3 alignments the Monarchy names form a coherent secondary group: the peak −9758-07-08 (|O|=4) reads 94 Monarchy names (31% of its 302 biblical names) including Abigail, Achimelek, Jerachmeel, Shealtiel, Malkiram, Achishachar — the Davidic/royal-line onomasticon — alongside the dominant Patriarchs/Conquest core (155, 51%). So a single rare alignment surfaces a coherent David/Solomon-era name cluster; Patriarchs/Conquest remains the overall dominant period on every peak, but the secondary clusters show the correspondence reaches every biblical period, each as a coherent group on the alignments whose occupied letters select it.

Non-recurrence (stellar-driven, anti-random). The long (len≥5) biblical names co-occur at avg 11.58 per peak (100% of peaks with ≥3 together); 31 of the 50 longest readable names appear on a single one of the 12 dated conjunctions only. Their recurrence gaps run to 5394 y (Marsena), with 23 gaps ≥2000 y, 39 ≥1000 y, 75 ≥500 y. A specific letter-cluster |O| reassembles only when the planets return to the same sign-occupancy — astronomically millennial — so the group appears once and vanishes; a uniform random draw would keep a regular baseline frequency. The clustered millennial-gap bursts are the anti-random, stellar-driven signature.

Null B — destroying name identity collapses the signal; reassigning signs does not (lexicon-level design localized). Two nulls now bracket the design layer. The existing sign-shuffle null reassigns each letter to a random sign (keeping |O| fixed): it preserves the biblical-proper rate at ~1× — a random letter→sign mapping reads the same high biblical rate. The new lexicon letter-shuffle null (Null B, null_lexicon.mjs, 5 seeds) instead shuffles the consonants within each lexicon entry — preserving length, distinct-letter count |S|, per-letter frequency and part-of-speech, but destroying name identity: under it, the name_refs concordance lookup fails on the shuffled strings, and the biblical-proper count collapses to 0.116× the real lexicon on the 12 dated conjunctions (8.7× above the chance-collision floor) and the long (len≥5) biblical count to 0.030× (~34× above floor). Destroying onomastic identity collapses the biblical signal; reassigning signs does not. The design is therefore localized to the lexicon — the alphabet forged as the biblical onomasticon — not to the letter→sign assignment.
