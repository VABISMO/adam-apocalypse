9.7 Hebrew calendar mnemonics and eclipse seasons

The Sefer Yetzirah does not predict eclipses, and the Sefer Raziel HaMalakh (p. 144: "combina los signos y la rueda… calcula los períodos… calcula en orden para ver las generaciones") gives the letter-astronomy scaffold but no eclipse algorithm. The real calculational engine for eclipses in the Jewish world is the Hebrew calendar, and its constants are the SY's constants — which is why the stellar alphabet can serve as a mnemonic for eclipse seasons even though it is not a physical model.

The molad is a conjunction engine. The Hebrew molad (mean lunar conjunction) = 29 d 12 h 793 parts = 29.530594 d, almost exactly the synodic month (29.530589 d; Δ ~0.4 s). A solar or lunar eclipse is a conjunction (new moon) or opposition (full moon) that occurs close enough to a lunar node. So the very quantity the molad tracks is the quantity that produces eclipses: an eclipse is a molad (or molad + ~14.77 d) that falls near a node. The Hebrew calendar does not predict the node — but it computes conjunctions to the part (1/18 minute), and a calendar that knows conjunctions knows when to look for an eclipse.

Sources that the paper had not cited (the Jewish calendar-astronomy tradition that makes these mnemonics operative, not merely decorative): the molad and tekufot (the seasons of Shmuel and of Rabbi Ada); the Baraita of Samuel —the single Jewish source that gives a lunar-node cycle and a quantified eclipse reckoning—; Maimonides, Mishneh Torah, Hilkhot Kiddush HaChodesh (the mean lunar motion that underlies the molad); and Ibn Ezra, Sefer ha-Olam (the 28 lunar mansions and precession, the Arabic manāzil adapted into Hebrew astronomy). These are the texts that turn "19 = 7 + 12" from a numerological pun into a calendrical tool: the 19-year cycle with its 7 leap years is the actual intercalation rule of the Hebrew calendar, and the 7 leap years are 7 in number.

Bounded claim. The stellar alphabet does not predict individual eclipses (date, path, magnitude); it cannot — it has no node model and no latitude. What its constants do is structure eclipse seasons: the molad + the 19-year cycle (7 leap = 7 doubles) is a real conjunction engine, and 19, 28, and 223 are the actual periods of the eclipse problem. The SY's 3/7/12 partition is therefore a mnemonic for the calendrical arithmetic that locates eclipse seasons, not a physical theory of eclipses. No claim is made that the SY predicts eclipses; the claim is that its numbers are the numbers of the calendar that does.
