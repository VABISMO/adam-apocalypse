1. Introduction and declaration of scope

The Sefer Yetzirah (SY, "Book of Formation", 2nd–6th c. CE) sets out a linguistic cosmogony: the universe was formed with 22 letters —3 mothers (א מ ש, elements), 7 doubles (ב ג ד כ פ ר ת, planets and dual dimensions) and 12 simples (ה ו ז ח ט י ל נ ס ע צ ק, constellations and months)—, and it states that with these 22 letters He formed the whole of His creation. The SY is cosmogonic linguistics: it binds the alphabet to the sky and makes the letter the instrument of formation.

This work takes the letter→luminary mapping of the SY as a verifiable protocol over astronomical ephemerides and poses the design question directly: is the structure of the Hebrew script reflected, measurably, in the structure of the sky, and does that reflection rise above a null model? The interest is strictly formal and empirical: given the mapping and an explicit rule, what regularities emerge and are distinguishable from a null model? Every numerical claim of the article is reproducible with the companion scripts.

In its historical form this stellar-letter reading was a divinatory technique, and the article documents it as such rather than denying it. It is attested across cultures — in the Persian talismanical tradition that Gaffarel records (1629/1650, Part IV: “it is Possible to reade [in the starres] whatsoever of Importance is to happen throughout the Universe”), in the combinatorial 231 gates of the Sefer Yetzirah, and in the Sefer Raziel's instruction to “compute the planets and the signs… to see the generations… [and] decree upon each one, between good and evil” (§15c.8, §15c.10). The apparatus of this study replicates that technique from those textual instructions. What the article does not do is claim to demonstrate that the technique predicts the future; it documents that it was used as divination — the texts say so — and shows that the intentional design of the script, established statistically in the sections below, is what makes such a stellar reading structurally coherent.

15c.11 Rare alignments of centuries and millennia, and the stellar reading on them

The decisive question for a dynamical reader of the sky is what role the rare configurations — the grand conjunctions that recur on the scale of centuries or millennia — play in the value of a stellar reading. An ordinary day already admits a reading fairly often, so an ordinary reading carries little individual probabilistic weight; a rare alignment, by concentrating many bodies in one zodiacal sign, discards most of the letter-combinations the reading rule would otherwise allow, so a reading that does arise on a rare alignment is itself a rare conjunction of two independent rare events and is the kind of reading that matters. Two questions follow: do alignments filter (discarding combinations, raising the significance of any surviving reading)? and do they generate (producing a reading that arises only on those days)? Two offline scans (scripts/calc_alignments.mjs, astronomy-engine v2.1.19, geocentric apparent ecliptic longitude, noon UT) address both directly. SCAN A covers the app's 10 bodies, 1700–2200 CE daily (the Pluto-valid range); SCAN B covers the 7 classical bodies — accurate over millennia — from −1000 to 2200 CE in a 3-day step. For each day two rarity metrics are computed: maxInSign = the most bodies in a single zodiacal sign, and span = the smallest arc (°) containing every body; each rare day is tagged with its precessional era (~2147 y each, §3) and its stellar reading (Genesis 1:1 legibility, readable-name count, Shem HaMephorash angel-roots).

Three rarity scales emerge. (i) Decades: across 500 years the 10-body set never reaches 8-in-one-sign — the maximum is 7 (e.g. 1962-02-04, 7 in Aquarius), and 7-in-sign events recur every few decades, not centuries. (ii) Centuries: the 7 classical bodies all in one zodiacal sign occurs 8 times in 3200 years (−244, −144, −46, 531, 907, 1662, 1962, 2000), with irregular gaps of 38–756 y — the famous grand conjunctions, on a centuries scale. (iii) Millennia: the tightest classical conjunctions — all 7 classical planets within the smallest arc found in the whole scan — are −144-07 (Cáncer, 16.3°) and 1962-02 (Acuario, 16.5°), separated by 2106 y ≈ 0.98 precessional era. These two tightest grand conjunctions fall in adjacent precessional eras (Aries → Pisces): the tightest classical alignment of an era sits near its boundary, ~one age (2147 y) from the tightest of the next. The precessional cadence (§3) is thus imprinted on the rarest classical conjunctions.

The alignment is a rarity filter — measured apples-to-apples. The reading rule is positional: a name is readable only when its simple-letter set S fits inside the set O of zodiacal signs actually occupied by a body (S ⊆ O, §10; the probability is q|S|, exponential in the number of distinct letters). A grand conjunction concentrates bodies in one sign, which shrinks O; the smaller O, the fewer sets S fit inside it, so most letter-combinations — and the names built from them — are discarded. The scans confirm this with each body set compared against its own ordinary 2024–2030 baseline. Under the 10-body set: Genesis 1:1 is legible 11.46% of ordinary days (293/2557, about one day in nine; avg 2031 readable names) but 0% of the 16 maxInSign≥7 rare days (0/16; avg 971 names). Under the 7-classical set: Genesis is already near-zero ordinarily (0.08%, 2/2557; avg 1486) and 0% on the 13 498 rare-alignment days (maxInSign≥5 or span≤60°; avg 826). The filter is real in both arms; the 10-body arm shows it most starkly, because there the ordinary baseline is substantial (one day in nine). The earlier “0.3% / 38-fold / 1408” figures mixed the 10-body ordinary baseline with the 7-classical rare rate and are withdrawn in favour of these apples-to-apples numbers.

For the tightest readings the filter is total, not merely rare — but only on the |O|=1 days, and only for multi-letter sets. Genesis 1:1 needs five distinct simple-letters (י ה ל ו צ — Virgo, Aries, Libra, Taurus, Aquarius). On any all-7-in-one-sign day O has exactly one member, so Genesis is provably never legible there (0/8 such days in −1000..2200: |O|=1 cannot cover a five-letter set). The earlier statement that a 7-in-sign Genesis reading arises on ~2×10⁻⁸ of all days was an artifact of mixing the 10-body ordinary baseline (11.5%) with the 7-classical rare rate: the clean 7-classical joint probability is zero, not one in fifty million. A 7-in-sign alignment does not make a Genesis reading rare — it makes it impossible. This |O|=1 collapse is specific to the tightest all-7-in-one-sign days and to names whose required letter-set is larger than one: on those days only single-simple-letter names (and the |S|=0 always-readable set) are readable. The broader rare set — 7 classical bodies within a 120° arc, where |O| ranges from 2 to 5 — does not collapse to zero; it reads as a substantial set of biblical names and places (next paragraph). The consonant-shrink is graded by how many bodies sit in one sign: the 10 dated conjunctions show |O|=1 on every 7-body event (−8267, −4018, −4831, −144, 1962), |O|=2 on the 6-body event (−958), and |O|=2–3 on the 5-body events — so the probability any specific |S|≥1 name is readable, P=q|S| with q=|O|/22, falls from ~0.14 (|O|=3) to ~0.045 (|O|=1): a |S|=2 biblical name is ~1.9% likely on a 5-body conjunction but only ~0.2% on a 7-body one. The tighter the alignment, the fewer the consonants and the more improbable every multi-letter biblical reading on it — which is why a long biblical name that does appear on a tight conjunction is itself the rare event.

The alignment filters; it does not generate new angel-roots — but it does surface a high, stable rate of biblical names and places, with a non-recurring multi-letter core. The same scans show that exactly the same Shem HaMephorash angel-roots are readable on rare-alignment days as on ordinary days — 0 new roots arise exclusively on rare alignments (13 under 7-classical, 9 under 10-body, all subsets of the ordinary 13). So alignments filter; they do not generate new angelic content. 16. Limitations

  5. Lexicon. Strong (OpenScriptures), 6045 consonantal roots + 290 curated Spanish glosses; the rest English. Incomplete for post-biblical Hebrew.
  6. Borderline palindrome result. The Genesis 1 nucleus palindrome excess (§13.3) is borderline (p ≈ 0.076, not significant at p < 0.05) and not multiple-comparisons-corrected; it is a weak hypothesis, not a proof.
