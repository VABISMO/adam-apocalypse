# Abū Maʿshar (Albumasar) — De Magnis Coniunctionibus (Latin, 1489 incunabulum)

`abu_mashar_de_magnis_coniunctionibus_1489_latin.txt` — the complete Latin text
of Abū Maʿshar's *De Magnis Coniunctionibus* ("On the Great Conjunctions"),
*De annorum revolutionibus* ("on the revolutions of the years"), and *De eorum
profectionibus* ("on their profections"), in the Latin translation by
**Johannes Hispalensis** (John of Seville), as printed in the 1489 Augsburg
incunabulum edited by **Johannes Angelus** and printed by **Erhard Ratdolt**.

The full title (from the title page): *Albumasar de magnis coniunctionibus,
annorum revolutionibus, ac eorum profectionibus: octo continet tractatus* —
"eight tractates." The work is the classical astrological treatise on great
planetary conjunctions (the Saturn–Jupiter great conjunctions in particular)
and their mundane/historical significance, the foundational text of
conjunction astrology in the Latin West.

## Edition
- Incunabulum: Augsburg, Erhard Ratdolt, 1489 (Johannes Angelus, editor).
- Source manuscript: Biblioteca Nazionale Centrale di Firenze, Magl. A.5.59.
- The OCR text retains the period's Latin spellings and heavy Scribal
  abbreviations/ligatures (e.g. `coiunctionib^` = *coniunctionibus*,
  `annoznm` = *annorum*, `reuolutionib^` = *revolutionibus*); it is a
  diplomatic-quality transcription of the early print, not a modernized
  normalized edition.

## Completeness evidence (verified complete)
- The title page announces the *octo continet tractatus* (eight tractates).
- The text carries through to the **eighth tractatus** ("…de completione…
  tractatus octavo nobis completibus… completimus totius libri").
- It closes with the proper colophon: *"Apud Albumasaris de magnis coniunctionibus
  explicit feliciter: magistri Iohannis Angeli viri peritissimi & diligentis
  correctione: Erhardus Ratdolt… Auguste… anno Domini 1489"* — i.e. "Here ends
  Albumasar's *De magnis coniunctionibus* happily… printed at Augsburg, 1489."
  (The OCR renders this as `ppud albumajaris &e magnis coiunctionib^ cjrpKcit
  feliciter… 14S9`.)

### Note on the digitization
The ProQuest *Early European Books* scan places an image-rights notice on each
page image; that notice pertains only to the digital page images, not to the
underlying 1489 text (which is public domain) or to this machine-generated OCR
text derivative. Those notice/watermark lines (and the repeated shelfmark
banner "Magl. A.5.59 / Firenze") have been stripped from this file so it
contains only the text of the work. A separate, incomplete 1515 Augsburg
edition also exists on archive.org (Getty copy, last leaf M6 wanting); the 1489
incunabulum used here is complete, ending with the proper colophon.

## Relevance to the project
*De Magnis Coniunctionibus* is the authority on planetary conjunctions and
mundane astrological cycles in the medieval/Latin tradition — the technical
astrological complement to the cosmological letter–planet–sign system the
paper documents. It transmits the framework of great conjunctions, revolutions,
and profections by which planetary cycles were read onto history, the same
cyclical astronomy (Saturn–Jupiter great conjunctions ≈ 20-year cycle; the
triplicity shifts ≈ 240 / 800 / 960 years) that grounds the project's
precession-era reading of the alphabet forged from celestial symbols.
