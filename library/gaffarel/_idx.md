# Gaffarel — *Unheard-of Curiosities* (1650, EN)

Jacques Gaffarel (1601–1681), *Curiositez inouyes sur la sculpture
talismanique des Persans* (Paris 1629); Englished by Edmund Chilmead,
London: G.D. for Humphrey Moseley, 1650.

Source: archive.org `b30333817` (Wellcome/Medical Heritage Library scan),
OCR full text → `_dl_ocr.txt` (raw, noisy 17th-c. long-s). Cleaned key
passages below. Cross-checked against the Wikisource structure stub and the
EEBO-TCP transcription (Univ. Michigan, CC0). Public domain.

## Structure (4 parts)

- **I.** Wherein the Jews, and other Eastern men are defended (against the
  charge of idolatry — their astronomy/letters are rehabilitated).
- **II.** Of the Talismanical Sculpture of the Persians; or the manner of
  making Figures, and Images, under certain Constellations.
- **III.** Of the Horoscope of the Patriarchs: or the Astrology of the
  Ancient Hebrews.
- **IV.** Of the Reading of the Stars, and what ever else is seen in the
  Aire.  Ch. XII (meteors/comets as celestial letters) + Ch. XIII (the
  stars ranged in the heavens in the form of Hebrew letters; reading the
  future there).  → the chapter directly relevant to the Caeli Reader
  thesis: stars = Hebrew letters = celestial writing.

Two folding plates: "Tables of the Celestiall Constellations, expressed
by Hebrew Characters."
